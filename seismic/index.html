In Baltimore, seismic design extends well beyond structural codes into the subsurface, where the Atlantic Coastal Plain sediments and deep Chesapeake Group strata can dramatically alter ground motion. Our seismic category addresses these local effects through seismic amplification analysis, evaluating how soft soils, fill, and weathered rock increase shaking intensity relative to competent bedrock—a critical step under ASCE 7 and IBC provisions adopted in Maryland. Equally important is site response analysis, which quantifies frequency-dependent amplification and helps engineers avoid resonance issues that linear static methods miss.

These studies are required for high-occupancy buildings, essential facilities, and infrastructure on marginal ground, including port expansions and retrofits of aging waterfront structures common in the Inner Harbor area. Whether your project involves deep foundations or liquefaction-prone silts, integrating seismic amplification analysis with a rigorous site response analysis ensures compliance with local building officials and delivers performance-based design confidence.
